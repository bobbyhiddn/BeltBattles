BeltBattles Privacy Policy

Last updated: 28 October 2026

This policy is for the game BeltBattles on iPhone, on Android and on the web at beltbattles.fly.dev. It says what the game keeps on your device, what it sends to its server, why, who can see it, how long it is kept and how you delete it. The game has no account, no sign-in and no in-app purchase, and it has no advertising and no analytics.

In short

  • Without a connection the game sends nothing, and plays against stand-in captains.
  • Online, the game sends the name you chose and your builds, so that other players can meet them as ghosts, together with a random player ID and your progress. If you link devices it also sends your run history.
  • The server deletes your ghosts 180 days after it received them, and the rest of your online data after 24 months without play.
  • You can delete your online data in the game: OPTIONS, then DELETE MY ONLINE DATA.

1. Who is responsible and how to reach us

BeltBattles is published by CrewPort Games. We decide why and how the data described here is used.

For any question about your data, write to micah@crewport.ai. The support page has the same address and short answers to common questions.

2. What the game keeps on your device

  • Your game: your run in progress, your profile (counters such as runs and wins, the heroes you won with, the looks you unlocked and chose), your settings (sound, reduced motion, speed), the tips you have seen, your personal best, a list of your latest finished runs (up to 50) and a pool of your own recent builds (up to 300).
  • The name you chose: 2 to 10 letters, digits, spaces or hyphens.
  • A random local ID. It never leaves your device.
  • Your signing key. The first time the game starts, even with no connection, it makes a key pair on your device (ECDSA P-256, with the cryptography built into your browser or phone). The game signs what it sends with the private key. Your player ID is a SHA-256 hash of the public key: a code, not your name. On the web the private key is stored so that it cannot be read or exported (in the browser's IndexedDB, database beltbattles-identity). In the iPhone and Android apps a copy of the private key is also kept in the app's private preferences storage (UserDefaults on iPhone, SharedPreferences on Android), so that you stay the same player if the system clears the app's web storage. That storage is private to the app, but it is not the Keychain or the Keystore.
  • Your link record, if you link devices: which player this device belongs to. The apps copy it to the same preferences storage.

The game uses the storage of your browser or app, not cookies. It does not ask for your location, contacts, photos, camera, microphone or advertising ID. Deleting the app removes all of this from the device, and on the web clearing the site's data does.

Offline: while your device reports that it has no connection, the game does not look for ghosts and does not upload builds or progress, and it plays against stand-in captains. The apps have no setting that turns online play off; to play without sending anything, use your device's airplane mode. (On the web, adding ?server=off to the address does the same for that visit.)

3. What the game sends, and when

The game sends data only to https://beltbattles.fly.dev, over HTTPS, and nowhere else. (The web page can be pointed at another server only by adding ?server= to its address by hand, a developer setting. The apps cannot.) The game makes no other network requests than these (a browser or an app may send a short preflight request, OPTIONS, before some of them):

  • Finding your opponent, GET /v1/ghosts/match: each time a day's dusk fight needs a ghost. It sends the rules version, the day, your wins and hearts in this run, your player ID, the IDs of the ghosts you already met in this run, how many runs you have finished, and whether the fight is ENDLESS. It carries no name and no signature.
  • Your build, as a ghost, POST /v1/ghosts: once for each dusk, before the fight, when the build passes the game's own check. It sends your build exactly as it fights (the pieces on your cruiser and how the belts connect them), your hero, the rules version, the day, your wins and hearts going into the fight, the name you chose, your player ID, a random run ID, the time on your device's clock and a few details the server uses to check that the build is legal: the run's random seed, the roads you took, your losses, your LAST CHANCE choice, whether it is your first run, and your ENDLESS wins, hearts and victory day. The server adds the time it received the ghost.
  • Your profile, PUT /v1/profile: when a run ends and after you change a look in CUSTOMIZE, one try each. It sends your counters (runs ended, runs won or completed, flawless runs, dusk wins, best day, warlord wins, matriarch wins, most belts), the heroes you have won a fight with and the heroes you have finished a run with, the looks you unlocked and chose, and the time of the last change. It is at most 2 KB. Your personal best stays on your device.
  • Reading your profile back, GET /v1/profile: only on a device that has shown or entered a link code, or linked a Steam account. It runs when the game starts, at most once an hour (the answer to ENTER A CODE already carries your profile). It sends the time on your clock and receives your player ID and your profile.
  • Linking two devices, POST /v1/link/code when you tap SHOW MY CODE (it sends the time on your clock and the server makes a one-time code for your player) and POST /v1/link/redeem when you tap ENTER A CODE on the other device (it sends the code you typed and the time). The server then records that the second device belongs to your player.
  • Steam, POST /v1/link/steam: not part of the iPhone, Android or web versions. A Steam version of the game can link your Steam account to your player. Its request carries a one-time ticket from Steam. The server checks the ticket with Steam's web service (run by Valve) and stores your Steam ID next to your player ID. The server accepts this only when its operator has switched Steam linking on.
  • Your run history, PUT /v1/history and GET /v1/history: only on a device that has shown or entered a link code, or linked a Steam account. Showing a code is enough, even if nobody enters it; from then on the device sends the runs it already holds (up to 50, ten at a time) and each new run, until you delete your online data. A run holds the hero, the rules version, a random run ID, the outcome, the wins, the days, the hearts left, the time the run ended and, for each dusk, its result and (when you played online) the record of the fight, which holds both builds as they fought. It holds no name, no address and no key. The device also reads the list of your player's runs back, so that your linked devices hold the same history. A device that has done neither sends none of this.
  • Deleting your data, DELETE /v1/player: when you confirm DELETE MY ONLINE DATA (section 7). It sends the time on your clock.

Every request except the opponent search is signed with your key, and carries your public key and the signature as headers. The server uses them to check the request and does not store them. When you open the web version, your browser also asks the server for the game page, and for this policy and the support page. The PRIVACY row in OPTIONS opens this policy in your browser in the same way.

With every request the server sees your IP address, which it needs to answer you. It holds the address in memory to limit how often one address can call, and writes it neither to its storage nor to its log. The request log holds the time, the method, the path, the status and the duration of each request. It holds no IP address, no query, no player ID, no key and no signature.

4. Why we use it

  • To pick an opponent of about your level: the opponent search uses the day, your wins and hearts and your finished runs.
  • To let other players meet your build: that is what a ghost is. Your name goes with it, so they see who built it.
  • To keep your progress together when you link devices: the profile, the link records and the run history.
  • To make sure that only you can write under your player ID: the signatures.
  • To keep the service working and safe: the server checks every build against the game's rules before it stores it, limits how often one address can call, and keeps the request log.

If you are in the EU, the UK or a similar place, the legal reasons are these: we need the data to run the online features you use (performance of the service), and we have a legitimate interest in keeping the service safe and working (the checks, the limits and the log). We do not use your data for advertising, for tracking you across other apps and sites, or to sell it.

5. Who sees it

  • Other players, and anyone who asks the server. Your own builds become ghosts for other players to meet. The opponent search needs no key and no sign-in, so a program other than the game can also ask for ghosts, and it gets what a player's game gets. When the opponent search picks your ghost, the answer holds the data of your upload listed in section 3, including your player ID and run ID, but not your key, not your signature and not the run's random seed. The game shows other players your name with a short tag made from your player ID (like ANNA#4F), your hero, your wins and hearts and your build. If you change your name in OPTIONS, later uploads carry the new name; ghosts already uploaded keep the name they had.
  • Our hosting providers process the data for us. Fly.io runs the server, and Tigris, the object storage that Fly.io offers, holds the stored data. They also keep their own technical records of connections, under their own policies.
  • Valve (Steam) sees the ticket, only in a Steam version that links a Steam account.
  • Apple and Google, and any other store where the game is sold, handle your purchase and download under their own privacy policies. We receive no payment details.
  • We do not sell your data and we do not share it for advertising. The game has no advertising, analytics or tracking code, and no code from other companies that collects data.

6. How long it is kept

The server keeps your online data until you delete it, with two time limits: it deletes your ghosts 180 days after it received them, and it deletes the rest of your online data after 24 months (730 days) without play. A clean-up that runs once a week does the deleting, and it also removes the stored ghosts of players who deleted their online data. The clean-up can run late or fail, so we promise only that a deletion that is due is done within 14 days.

Deletion recordAfter you delete your online data, the server keeps one small record: your player ID and the IDs of your linked devices (codes made by hashing, not names) and the time of the deletion. It has no end date. It is what keeps your old ghosts from being served again.

Without play means that for 24 months (730 days) the server has seen none of these from you or from a device linked to you: a ghost or a profile it received, a run in your run history, or a link (linking a device or a Steam account, or showing a link code). Playing without a connection sends nothing, so it does not count.

On your device, data stays until you delete the app or clear the site's data.

7. How to delete your online data

  1. On the title screen tap OPTIONS.
  2. Tap DELETE MY ONLINE DATA. The row is greyed out while the game has no online session, and a line under it says why.
  3. The card says THIS DELETES YOUR ONLINE PROFILE, YOUR LINKS AND YOUR GHOSTS. and YOUR PROGRESS ON THIS DEVICE STAYS. Tap DELETE. (CANCEL leaves everything as it was.)
  4. When it has worked, the card says DONE. YOUR NEXT ONLINE GAME STARTS A NEW PLAYER.

Without a connection the card cannot reach the server and says NO ANSWER FROM THE SERVER. TRY AGAIN. If it says YOUR CLOCK IS WRONG. SET IT RIGHT AND TRY AGAIN., set your device's clock and try again.

What it removes at once

Your online profile, the links between your devices and any Steam link, a link code you have not used, and your run history. Your ghosts stop being served from that moment (at most a minute later if the server runs on several machines), so nobody can meet them.

What it keeps

The deletion record described in section 6. On each of your devices, your local progress, settings, name and list of finished runs stay. The device you deleted from throws away its key and link record, and makes a new player at your next online game.

Players who already met your ghost keep what their game saved: your build inside their fight records (which are also on our server, in their run history, if they linked devices) and, until their saved run is replaced, your name, player ID and run ID in their saved game. Deleting your data does not reach those copies.

If you keep playing online

After you restart the game, your next online game sends your name, builds and progress again, under a new player ID. To stop that, play with your device's connection off.

Your stored ghosts

The weekly clean-up removes the stored copies of the ghosts of every player who deleted their online data. It can run late or fail, so we promise only that it does this within 14 days after you delete your online data. Until it has run, those ghosts are stored but never served.

Your other devices

If you linked more than one device, the others find out the next time they send a ghost or a run: the server answers that the player was deleted. They then throw away their key and link record and start a new player. Their local progress stays.

If you cannot use the game

If your device is lost, wiped or sold, you cannot sign the request. Write to micah@crewport.ai and quote your player ID. The game shows it: tap OPTIONS, then MY PLAYER ID. It is a code like XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX, shown with the name and tag other players see you under. The row is greyed out while the game has no online session, as DELETE MY ONLINE DATA is. Note it while you still have the device. If you did not note it, tell us the name you used in the game, the hero and roughly when you played. We find the player and delete its online data the way the game does, its stored ghosts included. Anyone who met your ghost can learn your player ID, so we may ask you questions to be sure the request is yours. We answer within one month (section 8).

8. Your rights

You can ask us for a copy of the data we hold about you (access), for wrong data to be corrected (correction), for your data to be deleted (deletion; you can also do it yourself, section 7), and that we stop or limit using it (objection and restriction). Write to micah@crewport.ai. We answer within one month.

There is no account, so we can find your data only from your player ID or from the name you used. The game shows your player ID: tap OPTIONS, then MY PLAYER ID. Note it while you still have the device. We may ask you questions to be sure we have the right data.

If you think we use your data wrongly, you can complain to the data protection authority in your country.

9. Backups on your device

  • iPhone: iOS includes an app's UserDefaults in the backup of your device, so the copy of your key may be in your iCloud or computer backup. A restored iPhone may come back as the same player.
  • Android: the app allows Android's Auto Backup, so the Google backup of your phone may include the app's saved data, with the copy of your key. A restored phone may come back as the same player.
  • Web: the private key cannot be exported from the browser.

We have not tested what each system restores. A backup made before you deleted your online data can bring back the old key. The server then refuses it (it answers that the player was deleted), and the game makes a new player.

10. Children

BeltBattles is for people aged 13 and over and is not aimed at children. It asks for no age and no contact detail. The name you choose can be any 2 to 10 letters, digits, spaces or hyphens (the game refuses a short list of offensive words), and other players see it, so please do not use your real name. If you think a child under 13 has played online, write to micah@crewport.ai and we will delete that player's online data.

11. Security

  • Everything the game sends goes over HTTPS. The game refuses a server address that is not https (except one on your own machine).
  • Uploads are signed with a key made on your device. The server stores a ghost, a profile or a history entry only under the player ID of the key that signed it (or of the player that key is linked to), so nobody can write under your player ID without your key. A link code that you make lets one device join your player for 10 minutes, so show a code only to a device of your own.
  • On the web the private key is stored so that it cannot be exported. In the apps a copy is kept in app-private storage, not in the Keychain or Keystore, so it is not protected by hardware.
  • The server checks every build against the game's own rules before it stores it.
  • A link code has 8 characters, works once and for 10 minutes, and is stored only as a hash. After 5 wrong tries from one address in 10 minutes, the server refuses more.
  • The server does not keep IP addresses in its storage or its log.

No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot promise it.

12. Where the data is processed

The server runs on Fly.io in Ashburn, Virginia, in the United States (Fly.io region iad). The data is stored in Tigris. We did not restrict where Tigris places objects, so by its default (a global bucket) objects are stored close to where they are written and copies can be made in other regions when they are read. If you live outside the United States, your data is therefore sent to the United States and may be copied to other countries. For questions about this, write to micah@crewport.ai.

13. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we change the date at the top of this page. The address stays the same: https://beltbattles.fly.dev/privacy.
